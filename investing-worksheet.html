Part 1: The Core Fundamentals of Investing ("Before You Trade")

Answer the questions below to establish your investment knowledge before deploying capital into the live market.

1. What is a Share?

Distinguish between depositing savings into an authorised bank account versus buying shares in a public company (e.g. BHP, CBA, Apple).

A share represents partial equity ownership in a corporation. Unlike bank deposits with guaranteed interest, shares fluctuate in price and provide returns via capital growth and dividends...

2. Two Forms of Investment Return

Define Capital Growth (capital gain/loss) and Dividends. How do investors realize each return?

Capital Growth: Selling shares for a higher price than purchased.
Dividends: Cash distribution of corporate profits paid directly to shareholders per share owned...

3. The Risk vs. Return Trade-Off

Rank the following assets from Lowest Risk (1) to Highest Risk (4):

[     ] Australian Government Bonds [     ] Bank Cash Term Deposit
[     ] ASX Blue-Chip Shares (BHP / CBA) [     ] High-Growth US Tech (NVDA / TSLA)
Rule of Thumb: Higher potential returns always require accepting higher potential volatility and downside risk.

4. Brokerage Fees & The Danger of "Churn"

In our simulator, each trade incurs a flat $10.00 AUD transaction fee. If you make 20 tiny trades worth $100 each, what percentage of your investment has vanished to fees alone?

Calculation: 20 trades × $10 = $200 total brokerage. Total capital traded: $2,000.
Percentage lost to fees: (______ / ______) × 100 = ______% fee drag!

The 25% Portfolio Diversification Cap

Our simulator strictly blocks any single share from exceeding 25% of your total portfolio ($12,500 max from an initial $50,000 cash balance). Why is this rule enforced?

Diversification cushions your portfolio against company-specific catastrophe or industry collapse. "Never put all your eggs in one basket."
